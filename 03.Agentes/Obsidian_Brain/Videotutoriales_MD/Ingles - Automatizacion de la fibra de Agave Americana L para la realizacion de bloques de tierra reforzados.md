**_Abstract_ —The automation industry in the construction sector has been a very important pillar for the planning and constant control of the fibers to improve the mechanical properties of the earth blocks. The present work implements automation as a way to obtain Agave Americana L. fiber, thus obtaining improvements in the mechanical properties of the earth blocks using the simple compression method, Brazilian diametral compression tensile method and the modulus of rupture method, using fiber dosages of 0%, 0.25%, 0.5%, 0.75% and 1%, these were expressed with respect to the total weight of the sample, on the other hand. For the process of Agave americana L fiber, LadVIEW software was used to simulate the automation, where there is an HMI screen, this uses a shredding machine to obtain 2 products, either fiber or juice of Agave Americana L and finally thanks to the conveyor belts this is directed to their respective warehouses. The result for the optimum compressive strength was given by using the dosage of 0.25% agave fiber increasing by 13.39% (2.54MPa), on the other hand, the tensile strength increased by 39.13% (0.32MPa) using 1% agave fiber, finally an increase of 14.26% (1.44MPa) was obtained in the flexural strength with 0.5% Agave americana L fiber.** 

### **_Keywords—Automation, earth blocks, mechanical properties, Agave fiber, Agave set._** 

## I. INTRODUCTION 

The construction sector is important for Peru's development, with a 4.15% increase in production by the end of the year 2022, evidencing the physical progress of works throughout the country [1]. The two most predominant materials used for housing construction are; in the first place, they are built with cement, which corresponds to 42.2%, and in second place are earthen constructions, which corresponds to 31.8% according to the National Institute of Statistics and Informatics of Peru (INEI) [2]. Earth-based constructions are making a comeback in recent times, since its presentation is less polluting, easy to recycle for new uses and its low cost, since it is freely available in most of the populations [3]. For a long time a way to improve the resistance of earth-based constructions is the reinforcement with fibers, currently there are many varieties of fibers from synthetic to vegetable fibers, which in turn many of them generate pollution and others are beneficial [4], but the use of vegetable fibers is more beneficial for its great sustainable contribution and low cost [5]. Agave americana L fiber has very beneficial mechanical 

properties, such as its high tensile performance compared to other fibers [6]. 

In recent research, such as the one carried out in the Philippines, the performance of the mechanical properties of soil blocks stabilized with cement in doses of 6%, 8%, 10% and 12% and at the same time combined with different fibers, such as coconut, abaca and mague fiber, was tested. It was determined that the fibers improve the compressive strength of the blocks with an increase of 33.79% [7]. Caballero also mentions in his research that bricks made only of raw clay have low flexural and compressive strength, so they used agave bagasse fiber Angustifolia haw with dimensions of 25 mm as reinforcement for the bricks, showing that it improves these mechanical properties significantly [8]. 

None of the present investigations present in depth the obtaining of agave fiber and its future use in the aspect of its industrialization, the demand for fiber to generate various uses, from handicraft products to elaboration as a derivative of construction material [9], On the other hand, the construction sector is growing every day, generating pollution in several aspects, due to poor planning of waste management and the lack of awareness in many cases of what to do with concrete demolitions, which is the reality in many Latin American countries, ending up with all this waste in rivers and lakes [10]. 

The use of automation is exploited in the construction sector by covering the reduced time in planning and cost of execution in projects from small to large scale [11]. Automation allows the extraction of fibers and to take advantage of the resources to generate construction materials [12], since the traditional management makes the sustainable materials industry grow slowly, the application of automation The present research aims to determine the maximum mechanical performance (compression, bending and tensile) of earth blocks reinforced with Agave americana L fiber, as well as the automation process for the extraction and transportation of Agave americana L fiber, with the advantage of taking advantage of the entire plant resource to generate more derivatives of sustainable and low-cost construction materials..

III. RESULTS 

To measure the mechanical resistance of the blocks, the parameters established by the Peruvian standard E080 and NTP 331.201 were followed, where the tests were carried out in the laboratory "QA/QC CONSTRUCCION SAC", guaranteeing the results of resistance to compression, bending and traction. 

## _A. Compression test results._ 

According to Fig. 7, it can be observed that the average compressive strength of all specimens exceeded the minimum strength (1 MPa). Likewise, the maximum compressive strengths of the average specimens were 2.27 MPa and 2.54 MPa using 0.25% and 0.5% Agave americana L fiber, respectively. On the other hand, the adobes without the addition of Agave americana L fiber had about 2.24 MPa, 

which generally have a lower compressive strength than those without the addition of Agave americana L fiber. 



Fig. 7. Compressive strength average. 

## _B. Bending test results._ 

According to Fig. 8, the average maximum flexural strengths achieved with the addition of Agave americana L fiber were 1.40 MPa and 1.44 MPa, giving an increase in strength using the specimens with 0.25% and 0.50% Agave americana L fiber. In addition, the blocks without the addition of the natural fiber had a low flexural behavior reaching 1.26 MPa. 



Fig. 8. Average bending resistance. 

## _C. Tensile test results._ 

From Fig. 9 it could be obtained that the average tensile strength of all the specimens managed to exceed the minimum strength (0.08 MPa), the maximum tensile strengths of the average specimens were 0.26 MPa and 0.32 MPa, which shows a great increase in strength, using 0.75% and 1.00% of Agave americana L fiber respectively; in addition, the blocks without the addition of Agave americana L fiber had low tensile behavior reaching 0.23Mpa.

## IV. DISCUSSIONS 

According to Peruvian regulations [17], which recommends the proportions of silt, clay and sand materials to be used to achieve the minimum mechanical resistance to compression 1.0 MPa and tensile 0.08 MPa, this allowed us to guarantee these resistances and even more to achieve a significant increase of the samples by adding the fiber of Agave americana L in different doses, in the same way, adding any other type of vegetable or synthetic fiber improves its resistance to compression notably [24]. The fiber size used is 25 mm which is efficient to improve the compressive and flexural strength as reported in the research [25], by reducing the pores of the soil block [25]. 

The results of the flexural strength which improved by 14.29% with respect to the standard sample using 0.5% of Agave americana L fiber (S+C+L+0.50A) disagree with another research [26], since their results indicate an improvement of 7.86% using the dosage of 0.75% of Agave Angustifolia Haw fiber. In addition, the compressive strength increased by 13.39% with respect to the standard sample using 0.25% of Agave americana L fiber (S+C+L+0.25A); however, the addition of 1.75% of cabuya fiber increased the compressive strength by 12% [27], these differences in both investigations are due to the use of a different species of Agave fiber. In addition, the use of natural fibers has incidence depending on the chemical composition, geometry and adherence for the improvement of its mechanical resistance in earthen blocks [28]. 

## V. CONCLUSIONS 

The mechanical resistance of the blocks was improved, since when compared to the standard sample (0% fiber), they presented an increase in their mechanical resistance. The addition of Agave americana L fiber increased the compressive strength by 13.39% (2.24 Mpa to 2.54 Mpa) with respect to the standard sample, the tensile strength improved by 39.13% (0.23 Mpa to 0.32 Mpa) with respect to the standard sample and the flexural strength by 14.29% (1.26 Mpa to 1.44 Mpa) with respect to the standard sample. 

According to the results, an improvement of up to 124% in compressive strength and up to 187.5% in tensile strength was obtained with respect to the minimum strengths established in Peruvian standard E.080.
